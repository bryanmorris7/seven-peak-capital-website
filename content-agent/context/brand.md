## 1. What @bryan7.ai Is

@bryan7.ai is one operator building a private equity firm in public — and teaching the AI systems he's building it with.

Two things happening at once, on purpose:

1. **The build.** Seven Peak Capital — multifamily equity and private credit. Raising, underwriting, closing, operating. Shown as it happens.
2. **The systems.** The AI workflows, agents, and stack running that business — taught in a way any small business owner or solopreneur can lift and use.

**The one-line version:**
I'm building a PE firm with AI running the back office. I show you the deals, and I show you the systems.

**Why the two connect:**
Most AI educators have never run a business under real pressure. Most operators never show their systems. The account sits in the gap — the proof and the playbook from the same person.

## 2. The Moat

Two layers, and they reinforce each other.

**Layer one — deal access.** T-12s, OMs, PPMs, rent rolls, LP updates, fund admin. Real documents from real transactions. Nobody in the AI content space has this.

**Layer two — live P&L.** Every workflow taught has been stress-tested inside a business with actual money, actual deadlines, and actual investors. Not a demo. Not a course example.

The filter for any idea:

> Am I teaching this because it works in my business, or because it sounds good?

If it hasn't run in Seven Peak or Peak AI, it doesn't get taught.

## 3. Who It's For

**Audience A — Operators, sponsors, GPs, LPs**
Real estate people doing real volume. They come for the deal content and the underwriting workflows. They convert to Seven Peak and the newsletter.

**Audience B — Small business owners and solopreneurs**
Agency owners, consultants, service businesses, one-person shops. They come for the AI systems — automation, agents, back-office replacement. They convert to Peak AI Real Estate and the broader education layer.

**Audience C — Founders and operators generally**
Watching someone build a firm from zero with modern tooling. They're here for the arc. They convert to newsletter and community.

**The unifying insight:** all three want the same thing — proof that this actually works when money is on the line. Serve that, and the audiences stop competing.

**Not for:** prompt collectors, hustle-culture audiences, "make $10k/month with AI" seekers, pure theory people.

## 5. Format Rules

- Video and reels only for pillar content. No carousels, no statics.
- Rotation: Screen Teardown, Desk POV, Walk-and-Talk, The Take, Overhead Hands.
- Screen recording beats talking head whenever the work is visible.
- Show the artifact. Document, dashboard, agent, terminal — put it on screen.
- Ring 1 skews Desk POV and Walk-and-Talk. Ring 2 skews Screen Teardown.
- Low-edit, sustainable, repeatable.

## 6. Voice

**Dark luxury editorial. Institutional but human.**

An operator explaining something to a peer across a desk. Never a creator performing for a camera.

### The rules

- Direct. No throat-clearing. No "in today's video," no "let's dive in."
- Confident without volume. Authority is specificity, not intensity.
- Short sentences. White space. Let statements land alone.
- Concrete over abstract. Numbers, documents, line items, real timelines.
- Understated when the result is impressive. Don't oversell your own work.
- Say what didn't work. Failed experiments are credibility.
- Teaching mode stays peer-to-peer — never talk down to the small business audience.

### Banned outright

- Exclamation points
- Emojis in professional content (use ✔️ where a mark is needed, never ✅)
- "Literally," "absolutely," "incredible," "game-changer"
- "Insane," "crazy," "wild," "unlock," "10x," "secret," "hack"
- "Listen up," "Nobody's talking about this," "This changed everything"
- Fake urgency, countdowns, manufactured scarcity
- Corporate hedging: "it could be argued," "generally speaking," "in many cases"

### Hook standard

Declarative and specific. Never curiosity-bait.

**On-brand — Ring 1:**
- "We're 40 days into this raise. Here's what's actually working."
- "This T-12 had a $340K problem on page four."

**On-brand — Ring 2:**
- "I replaced three hours of admin a day with one agent. Here's the build."
- "Most solopreneurs automate the wrong thing first."
- "Three tasks you should never hand to AI in a client business."

**Off-brand:**
- "You won't believe what AI just did to my deal 🤯"
- "The AI hack every business owner NEEDS to know"
- "POV: it's 2026 and you're still doing this manually"

### Structure default

1. Specific claim or artifact on screen
2. The actual work — visible, step by step
3. What it caught, saved, or produced, in real numbers
4. What it still gets wrong
5. Single CTA, only when earned

## 8. Compliance Guardrails

Seven Peak Income Fund II is a **506(c)** offering. General solicitation is permitted, but every purchaser must be verified accredited. That verification is the protection, not a disclaimer.

Antifraud rules including Rule 10b-5 apply to **all** public statements regardless of exemption type.

Hard rules for any content touching the fund:

- ✔️ No specific return projections without stated assumptions
- ✔️ No performance claims without stated methodology
- ✔️ No implied guarantees. No "safe." No "can't lose."
- ✔️ Past deal performance is never framed as indicative of future results
- ✔️ Fund-specific content gets securities counsel review before it goes live
- ✔️ Ring 2 and Ring 4 content stays educational — keep the line between teaching and offering clean

When in doubt, strip the fund reference and keep the education.

*Working guardrail, not legal advice.*

## 10. Output Contract

Anything generated under this brand must be:

- Copy-paste ready — no "here's a draft, adjust as needed"
- Free of every banned word and pattern above
- Grounded in a real artifact, workflow, or moment from the business
- Written the way Bryan talks, not the way a brand talks
