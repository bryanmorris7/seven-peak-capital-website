The Foundation

 

Plain English: how private real estate actually works.

No assumptions. Start from zero.

Every word the next 10 emails will use, defined right here. Save this one.

PRIVATE REAL ESTATE

Real estate that is not traded on a public stock exchange. You can't buy it on Robinhood. You buy it directly through a private offering, usually structured as an LLC.

 

SYNDICATION

A group of investors pooling money to buy a single property — usually an apartment building you couldn't afford alone. A $30M apartment community might have 50 investors putting in $50K to $1M each.

 

LP  ·  LIMITED PARTNER

That's you. You contribute capital. You don't manage the property. You receive distributions and a share of the profits at sale. Your liability is capped at what you invested.

 

GP  ·  GENERAL PARTNER (also: SPONSOR / OPERATOR)

The team running the deal. They find the property, raise the capital, secure the loan, manage the asset, and execute the exit. They get paid through fees and a share of the profits — but only after LPs hit a target return (the "preferred return").

 

PREFERRED RETURN ("PREF")

The minimum return LPs receive before the GP earns anything. If a deal targets an 8% pref, LPs get the first 8% of profits each year. Anything above that is split between LPs and GP — typically 70/30 or 50/50.

 

DISTRIBUTION

A check (or wire) from the deal to your account. Usually quarterly, sometimes monthly. Comes from rental income or interest payments.

 

506(c)

An SEC rule that lets sponsors publicly advertise a private deal — but only accept money from accredited investors. That's why we ask for accreditation verification (third party — Parallel Markets — handles it).

 

ACCREDITED INVESTOR

Income above $200K (or $300K with a spouse) for the past two years, or $1M+ in net worth excluding your primary residence. The SEC's threshold for "you can handle private market risk."

What Seven Peak Capital does.

We do two things. That's it.

01  ·  MULTIFAMILY EQUITY

We own apartment buildings.

We syndicate equity into apartment communities in growth markets. Investors own a piece of the building. They get quarterly cash flow from rents, plus a share of the profit when the property is sold or refinanced. Hold periods: typically 3–7 years.

 

02  ·  PRIVATE CREDIT

We lend to other operators.

Through Seven Peak Income Fund I, we make real estate-backed loans to other sponsors. Investors don't own the property — they own a piece of the debt. Interest payments come back as quarterly distributions. Shorter duration, more downside protection. The first vintage closed; the 2026 vintage opens with a waitlist (lesson 08).

Equity owns the upside.
Credit owns the downside protection.
