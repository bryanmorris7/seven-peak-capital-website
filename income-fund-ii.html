Investment Thesis

Built on what worked in Fund I.

Income Fund I taught us what our investors actually wanted: predictable distributions, an investor-first waterfall, and exposure to real estate without the wait of a five-to-seven-year equity hold. The structure resonated. The waitlist filled faster than capacity.

Income Fund II carries the same investor-aligned waterfall forward — with an upgraded underlying credit partner. The new partner is a senior-secured residential bridge lender with a two-year track record, zero principal losses across forty loans, and a tech-first operating model that compresses overhead and tightens underwriting.

The partner is at a meaningful inflection. An institutional bank facility activates as fund AUM crosses $10 million — materially expanding margin and capacity. Early Class A-1 investors are the capital that bridges that step-up, and Class A-1 economics reflect that recognition.

Investor FAQ

Common Questions

Income Fund II carries the same investor-aligned waterfall as Fund I — preferred return paid first to investors, 50/50 profit split above preferred, no management fee. The change is the underlying credit partner. Fund I deployed into a distressed-debt strategy. Fund II deploys into senior-secured residential bridge lending — a different risk profile with shorter duration, lower leverage at the loan level, and a 60% repeat-borrower base. Fund II also introduces a Class A-1 tier at the $250,000 minimum with a 12% preferred return.
Class A is the standard share class — $50,000 minimum with a 10% annualized preferred return. Class A-1 is structured for larger commitments at $250,000 minimum with a 12% annualized preferred return. Both classes receive a 50/50 profit split above their respective preferred return. Class A-1 capacity is capped to preserve allocation for early Class A-1 investors and to maintain blended fund economics.
The underlying operating partner is an institutional senior-secured residential bridge lending platform with a two-year track record and three principals based in New York. We disclose the partner's identity during the diligence call and in the Private Placement Memorandum, along with their full track record, principals' backgrounds, bank facility detail, and operational technology stack.
The minimum investment is $50,000 for Class A and $250,000 for Class A-1. Preferred return distributions are paid monthly via ACH or wire transfer. Profit-share distributions above the preferred return are paid quarterly. Distribution timing and amounts are subject to fund performance and the terms outlined in the Operating Agreement.
Income Fund II is structured as a Wyoming Limited Liability Company and is offered under Rule 506(c) of Regulation D promulgated by the SEC. Under 506(c), the fund may publicly advertise its offering but must verify that all investors are accredited. Seven Peak Income Fund II Manager, LLC serves as the manager of the fund. Full structural details, rights, and obligations are set forth in the Operating Agreement and Private Placement Memorandum.
Investor capital is deployed into a senior-secured residential bridge lending platform where every loan is collateralized by a first-lien position on real property. The operating partner underwrites positions with conservative loan-to-value ratios, typically at or below 75% of the property value. Each borrower also provides a personal guarantee. A credit reserve account at the operating fund holds partner capital in first-loss position, absorbing operating-fund losses before LP capital is impaired.
Income Fund II has a 12-month lock-up period from the date of each subscription. After the lock-up, investors may request redemption on a quarterly basis, subject to the terms of the Operating Agreement and available fund liquidity, which is in turn subject to liquidity at the underlying operating fund. Investors should review the full liquidity provisions in the Operating Agreement and PPM before investing.
Income Fund II is targeted to open for subscriptions on August 1, 2026. Soft commits are accepted now and reserve allocation — particularly important for Class A-1, which is capacity-capped. To be added to the early-access list, schedule an introductory call or submit your information through our contact form.
